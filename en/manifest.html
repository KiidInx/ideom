Manifesto

Not a corporation. A workshop.

What IDEom stands for, who it's built for, and why it doesn't belong to any corporation — honestly, without marketing.

IDEom isn't being made in an open-plan office. It's being made on one desk in Germany — built by one developer together with an AI partner, and tested on exactly the kind of device it's meant for: an ordinary Android phone. No super-device, no server farm. A phone, like the one in your hand.

Why at all

Programming shouldn't be a question of equipment.

Anyone who wants to build an app today normally needs equipment first: a laptop, a configured development environment, often a degree to explain how it all fits together. That's an invisible barrier — it isn't in the requirements, but it keeps most people away from the first line.

And it's rarely ideas that are missing. Almost everyone has a phone in their pocket and, at some point, the thought "there should be an app for that". IDEom is built for exactly that moment: for the beginner, the curious, the one who never had the access. Your first app should get to grow with your idea — not fail because you don't have the tools.

Human and machine

A human and an AI build this — openly said.

IDEom is built by a human together with an AI partner, and that's exactly how it's used: you describe, the AI works with you, you decide. The AI partner isn't a selling point printed on the box — it's part of the workshop, from the first idea to the finished app.

IDEom neither hides this nor oversells it. An AI makes mistakes, the way people make mistakes. That's why you keep the last word at every step — not out of distrust, but because collaboration means a human decides in the end. What that looks like in practice is on How it works.

Independent

No corporation — and that's on purpose.

There's no investor behind IDEom expecting a return, and no growth pressure that will eventually demand turning you into the product. There's no advertising, no dark patterns, no tricks pushing you to pay. What IDEom costs and why is stated openly on the plans page.

That has a price, honestly: a one-person workshop is slower than a team, some things are rough, and not every idea arrives right away. What you gain is that nobody has to be served except you. There are no interests in the background running against yours.

Transparency

Transparency isn't a feature, it's the build method.

Built in the open means: every significant change appears publicly as a workshop note on the home page and in the app — progress as well as setbacks. And what happens to your data is stated plainly on the security page and in the privacy policy — not hidden in 40 pages of legalese.

In the end IDEom is a promise to the person using it: I'm building the tool I wish I'd had when I started — and I'm building it in the open, so you can see what's inside. If something strikes you, is broken or missing, tell me. I read every report.

— Patrick, the IDEom workshop
